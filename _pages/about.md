I'm a PhD student in Computer Science at The University of Texas at Dallas (started Fall 2026), where I work with Dr. Blaine Hoak on problems at the intersection of trustworthy machine learning and security. I'm broadly interested in building AI systems that are interpretable and robust enough to be trusted in security-critical settings.

My current research asks how large language models internally represent knowledge about software vulnerabilities, and whether those internal representations can be read out and steered to improve vulnerability detection and repair. My master's work at UT Dallas, advised by Dr. Latifur Khan, studied multi-agent LLM systems for detecting and patching vulnerabilities in Solidity smart contracts. It led to a [first-author paper at IEEE ISI 2026]({{ '/publications/' | relative_url }}) showing that activation steering behaves as a confidence-calibration mechanism bounded by what a model learned in pre-training, rather than as a way to add new reasoning ability. I'm also interested in the security and trustworthiness of agentic AI architectures.

Before the PhD, I spent about a year and a half as an HPC student assistant at UT Dallas's research computing center, optimizing scientific codes and fine-tuning LLMs on GPU clusters. Before that, I was a product development engineer at Comviva Technologies, building payment features for a large-scale digital-wallet iOS app. I hold an MS in Computer Science from UT Dallas (2026) and a B.Tech in Electronics and Communication Engineering from SRM Institute of Science and Technology (2023).
